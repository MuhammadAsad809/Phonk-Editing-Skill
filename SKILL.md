# Phonk Video Editing Skill

## Role
You are an expert video editor specializing in the Phonk aesthetic. Create high-energy, beat-synced edits across major Phonk styles while maintaining clean pacing, intentional effects, and strong visual impact.

## Objective
Given footage and a Phonk track, produce a short-form edit with:

- Beat-synchronized cuts
- Strong drop/climax alignment
- Speed ramps and motion emphasis
- Controlled shake, blur, glitch, and VHS effects
- Style-specific color grading
- Consistent visual rhythm
- Platform-ready vertical export when appropriate

---

## 1. Phonk Style Reference

### Classic Memphis Phonk
- Slower, hazy, underground feel
- 1990s rap influence
- Lo-fi textures, film grain, VHS artifacts
- Long takes and hypnotic pacing
- Warm/desaturated grading

### Drift Phonk
- Aggressive, cowbell-driven style
- Common in car, gym, and short-form edits
- Fast cuts and hard drops
- Speed ramps, camera shake, motion blur
- High contrast with neon accents

### Brazilian Phonk
- Phonk production combined with Brazilian funk rhythms
- Fast, chaotic, energetic movement
- Rapid montage and strobe/glitch accents
- Saturated and vibrant grading

### Gym/Hype Phonk
- Heavy 808-focused energy
- Minimal melody and visual clutter
- Designed around powerful physical movements
- Strong impact cuts, zoom pulses, and controlled shake

## 2. Audio Analysis & Beat Mapping

### Track Selection
Prefer tracks with:
- A strong driving bassline
- Clear kick/808 hits
- Cowbell or recognizable rhythmic accents
- Vocal samples or memorable phrases
- A noticeable build-up and drop

### Beat Mapping
1. Import the music first.
2. Locate the kick, 808, cowbell, and major vocal hits.
3. Add beat markers manually or with automatic beat detection.
4. Place major cuts on strong beats.
5. Use shorter cuts during high-energy sections.
6. Use longer shots during build-ups so the drop has more impact.

For fast tracks, visual changes can occur roughly every `0.4–0.6s`, but the audio should remain the authority. Do not force cuts simply to hit a fixed time interval.

### Drop Structure
Build edits around:

`Build-up → tension → brief pause → drop → maximum visual impact`

At the drop, synchronize one or more major events:
- Main subject reveal
- Drift/impact
- Punch or movement
- Transformation
- Crash zoom
- Freeze frame
- Heavy shake

---

## 3. Core Editing Rules

### One Motion Rule
For AI-generated clips, use one dominant camera movement per short generation:
- Whip pan
- Rapid tracking
- Crash zoom
- Push-in
- Pull-out

Avoid combining several extreme camera movements in the same generated shot because it can increase visual artifacts.

### Cutting Rules
- Kick = primary cut point
- 808 = shake/zoom trigger
- Cowbell = speed-ramp or micro-cut trigger
- Vocal hit = text/overlay opportunity
- Drop = strongest visual event

### Pacing
- Build-up: 2–4 beats per shot
- Transition: 1–2 beats per shot
- Drop: 1 beat or half-beat cuts when the footage supports it

Do not cut every beat automatically. Use variation to prevent the edit from becoming mechanically repetitive.

---

## 4. Speed Ramping

Speed ramps create the characteristic velocity of many Phonk edits.

### Recommended Patterns
**Slow → Fast**
- Slow down before impact
- Snap into faster playback on the beat
- Best for reveals, drifts, punches, jumps, and transformations

**Fast → Slow**
- Build momentum
- Slow directly on the key action
- Best for emphasizing a pose, hit, or close-up

### CapCut Workflow
1. Select clip.
2. Open `Speed` → `Curve` / `Velocity`.
3. Start with a preset.
4. Edit keyframes around the beat.
5. Pair strong ramps with motion blur.
6. Preview at full speed to verify that the ramp lands on the music.

Use 2×–4× acceleration only when the source footage has enough movement to support it.

---

## 5. Camera Shake & Zoom

Shake should reinforce impact, not make the entire edit unstable.

### Beat Shake
At the beat marker:
- Scale: approximately `110–120%`
- Offset position slightly
- Return toward normal scale between impacts

For important hits, combine:
- Small position shake
- Brief zoom pulse
- Motion blur
- Short chromatic aberration

Use stronger shake only for major impacts.

---

## 6. Motion Blur

Motion blur helps rapid movement and transitions feel smoother.

### CapCut
- Apply motion blur to fast-moving clips.
- Start around `30–60%` and adjust according to footage.

### After Effects
- Enable layer motion blur.
- Use CC Force Motion Blur when additional blur is required.

Do not blur every clip. Static or slow shots usually benefit from remaining sharp.

---

## 7. Glitch, VHS & Chromatic Effects

Phonk edits often use controlled visual degradation.

### Useful Effects
- Chromatic aberration
- Film grain/noise
- VHS scan lines
- Tracking errors
- Color bleed
- Frame stutter
- Digital tearing
- Glitch flashes

### Layering Rule
Use several subtle effects instead of one extreme effect. A common combination is:

`Film grain + subtle chromatic aberration + short beat shake`

Reserve heavy glitches for transitions, drops, or specific accents.

---

## 8. Skull & Graphic Overlays

Skull imagery is commonly associated with drift/sigma-style Phonk edits.

### Workflow
1. Import a transparent overlay.
2. Place it above the main footage.
3. Use `Screen` or `Overlay` blending when available.
4. Start around `30–50%` opacity.
5. Animate scale, rotation, or opacity subtly.
6. Trigger the strongest appearance on a drop or accent.

The overlay should integrate with the footage rather than look pasted on.

## 9. Color Grading

### Base Phonk Grade
Starting range:

| Parameter | Starting Range |
|---|---:|
| Saturation | `-20 to -40` |
| Contrast | `+20 to +40` |
| Highlights | `-10 to -20` |
| Shadows | `-30 to -50` |
| Vignette | `+15 to +30` |

These values are starting points, not mandatory settings. Adjust according to the source footage.

### Drift Phonk
- High contrast
- Deep blacks
- Cyan/magenta accents
- Sharp highlights
- Dark overall mood

### Classic Memphis
- Lower saturation
- Warm/sepia bias
- Softer contrast
- Heavy grain
- VHS texture

### Brazilian Phonk
- Higher saturation
- Strong color separation
- Bright highlights
- Fast, energetic visual palette

### Gym/Hype
- Cool blue/teal bias
- Clean contrast
- High clarity
- Minimal decorative grading

## 11. CapCut Full Workflow

### Step 1 — Create Project
- New project
- Import all footage
- Set the intended aspect ratio

### Step 2 — Add Audio
`Audio → Sounds → From device`

Place the full Phonk track first so the timeline can be built around it.

### Step 3 — Mark Beats
`Audio → Beat → Add beat`

Use manual markers or automatic beat detection when available.

### Step 4 — Build the Rough Cut
- Place the strongest footage first.
- Cut to major beats.
- Keep build-up sections less crowded.
- Save the strongest shot for the drop.

### Step 5 — Add Speed Ramps
Use `Speed → Curve/Velocity` on movement-heavy clips.

### Step 6 — Add Impact Effects
Use only where the audio calls for them:
- Shake
- Zoom pulse
- Motion blur
- Flash
- Chromatic aberration

### Step 7 — Add Overlays
Add skulls, smoke, text, or other visual motifs only where they support the beat or story.

### Step 8 — Grade
Start with contrast and saturation adjustments, then create the specific subgenre look.

### Step 9 — Sound Design
Layer short effects such as:
- Whoosh
- Impact
- Reverse hit
- Bass hit

Keep them below the main music unless the effect is intentionally foregrounded.

### Step 10 — Final Review
Watch the edit twice:
1. With audio to verify beat synchronization.
2. Without audio to check whether the visual rhythm still makes sense.

### Step 11 — Export
For vertical short-form video:
- `1080 × 1920`
- `9:16`
- `30 or 60 FPS`
- High bitrate

60 FPS is useful when the edit contains speed ramps and smooth motion.

## 12. After Effects Workflow

1. Create a `1080 × 1920` composition.
2. Import footage and audio.
3. Add beat markers manually or with a beat-detection workflow.
4. Use Time Remapping for speed ramps.
5. Enable motion blur where appropriate.
6. Use controlled Position/Scale animation for camera shake.
7. Create chromatic aberration using channel separation or suitable effects.
8. Grade with Lumetri Color or an equivalent workflow.
9. Add overlays using Screen/Overlay blending.
10. Build glitch effects using Fractal Noise, Displacement Map, masks, or dedicated effects.
11. Export as high-quality H.264 or another platform-appropriate codec.

### Example AE Shake Expression
```jsx
wiggle(10,15)
```

Use small values first and synchronize the strongest movement with beat markers.

## 16. Common Mistakes

### Too Many Effects
Using every effect on every beat destroys hierarchy and makes the edit look amateur.

### No Build-Up
If every shot is intense, the drop has no contrast.

### Off-Beat Cutting
Cuts that consistently miss the kick/808 will make the edit feel disconnected from the music.

### Over-Grading
Excessive saturation, crushed shadows, and extreme sharpening can destroy footage detail.

### Overpowered Overlays
Skulls, glitches, and text should support the footage rather than cover it.

### Same Rhythm Throughout
Use variation. Combine longer shots with short impact cuts.

## 18. Editing Decision Logic

When editing any Phonk video, prioritize in this order:

`Music → Beat → Footage → Pacing → Speed → Impact Effects → Overlays → Color → Export`

If an effect makes the beat synchronization, subject visibility, or pacing worse, remove the effect.

## 9.2 AI Enhancement Tools

- **Topaz Video AI:** Upscale and enhance low-resolution footage when the source quality is limiting the final export.

### Enhancement Rule
Never use upscaling as a substitute for good source footage. Excessive sharpening or denoising can create halos, plastic-looking surfaces, and unstable details around cars.

- **Suno / Udio:** Generate original music concepts or tracks when the intended use and tool license permit it.

### Audio Rule
If the track contains a strong bass drop, prioritize the kick, 808/bass transient, and major section change as the primary timing anchors. Cowbell hits and secondary percussion should control micro-cuts, not every effect.

# 10. Manual + AI Hybrid Techniques

## 10.1 Velocity / Speed Ramping

AI can help identify high-motion moments, but the editor should control the final velocity curve.

Recommended structure:

1. Start close to normal speed.
2. Slow down immediately before the key visual moment.
3. Build acceleration into the beat/drop.
4. Snap back to normal or faster speed after impact.
5. Add motion blur only where it improves perceived motion.

Avoid constant 2x–4x speed. If every shot is extreme, no moment feels important.

## 10.2 AI Motion Tracking & VFX

Use tracking to attach graphics and effects to the car rather than floating them over the frame.

Examples:

- 3D text locked to the car's body or road surface
- Neon glow following headlights or taillights
- Sparks, smoke, dust, or particles following motion
- Directional light effects matching the environment
- Tracking-based HUD or speed graphics

### Tracking Quality Check
Review frames with rapid rotation, occlusion, wheel movement, reflections, and motion blur. These are common failure points for automated tracking.

## 10.3 AI Background Replacement

- **Runway/background-removal workflows:** Isolate the vehicle and replace the environment.
- **CapCut Auto Cutout:** Rapid subject isolation for mobile workflows.
- Add matching perspective, blur, grain, lighting, and shadows after compositing.

### Compositing Rule
The replacement background must match the original camera perspective, light direction, depth of field, and motion. A perfect cutout still looks fake when the lighting is inconsistent.

## 10.4 AI-Generated Visual Effects

Useful effect categories:

- Glitch/datamosh transitions
- RGB split/chromatic aberration
- AI-assisted stabilization followed by intentional beat-synced shake
- Generated smoke, sparks, rain, dust, and atmospheric particles
- Background extensions and cleanup

Do not stack all effects at maximum intensity. Use one dominant effect per impact moment and supporting effects at lower intensity.

# 11. AI-Heavy Audio Processing

## 11.1 Sound Design Stack

**Stage 1 — Beat Extraction**

Detect BPM, downbeats, kicks, bass hits, fills, transitions, and drops.

**Stage 2 — Stem Separation**

Separate the track into useful components where available:

- Bass/808
- Drums
- Vocals
- Melody/other

**Stage 3 — Audio Enhancement**

Clean noise, normalize inconsistent levels, and improve clarity without destroying the original character.

**Stage 4 — SFX Layering**

Add carefully chosen sounds such as:

- Engine rev
- Turbo spool
- Gear shift
- Tire screech
- Impact/whoosh
- Brake or mechanical clicks

**Stage 5 — Ducking / Automation**

Automate music and SFX levels so important samples and impacts remain clear. Do not flatten the entire mix by keeping everything loud.

### Audio Priority
**Beat → major impact → bass/808 → secondary percussion → transition SFX → atmosphere.**

# 12. End-to-End AI + Manual Workflow

Use this sequence for a high-energy Phonk car edit:

1. **Choose the track** — identify BPM, key sections, buildup, drop, and strongest bass hits.
2. **Analyze the audio** — generate or manually verify beat markers.
3. **Collect footage** — prioritize shots with obvious motion, headlights, wheels, steering, acceleration, drifting, and environmental movement.
4. **Create a rough cut** — place the best shots on major beats before adding effects.
5. **Build the drop** — reserve the strongest visual moment for the main drop.
6. **Add velocity** — use slow-in/fast-out or fast-in/slow-out ramps only where they increase impact.
7. **Add motion treatment** — motion blur, controlled shake, and directional movement.
8. **Track VFX** — attach text, glow, particles, or graphics to the vehicle/environment.
9. **Add transitions** — hard cuts first; use whip, blur, glitch, or crash zoom only when the motion justifies them.
10. **Grade the footage** — establish one consistent look before adding selective neon accents.
11. **Design the sound** — layer engine, tire, turbo, whoosh, and impact sounds without masking the music.
12. **AI enhancement pass** — upscale, denoise, stabilize, or generate inserts only where needed.
13. **Quality-control pass** — inspect every major cut, tracking point, generated frame, and audio impact.
14. **Export and review on a phone** — verify readability, compression, motion, and beat clarity at actual viewing size.

# 16. My Recommended Additions

These additions make the skill more useful as an actual editing agent rather than just a tutorial.

## 16.1 Edit Decision Rules

The editor should make decisions in this order:

1. **Music structure**
2. **Shot quality**
3. **Beat placement**
4. **Motion/velocity**
5. **Composition**
6. **VFX**
7. **Color**
8. **SFX polish**

Never use effects to compensate for weak footage or bad timing.

## 16.2 Beat-to-Effect Mapping

| Audio Event | Default Visual Response |
|---|---|
| Kick | Cut or small impact |
| 808 hit | Scale pulse / controlled shake |
| Cowbell | Micro-cut or transition cue |
| Snare/clap | Flash, directional movement, or text hit |
| Bass drop | Major transition / strongest shot |
| Vocal chop | Typography or subject emphasis |
| Silence/break | Hold, slow motion, or visual reset |

This is a starting rule, not a requirement for every track.

## 16.3 AI Prompt Structure for Generated Car Shots

Use this structure:

**Subject + action + environment + camera movement + lens/look + lighting + speed/motion + atmosphere + continuity constraints**

Example:

> Black JDM sports car drifting through a rain-soaked industrial street, rapid lateral tracking, low 24mm camera, neon reflections, high-contrast night lighting, realistic wheel motion, controlled tire smoke, cinematic motion blur, consistent vehicle design, no logo distortion, vertical 9:16 composition.

### Generation Rule
Generate short shots designed for a specific edit position. Do not generate random cinematic clips and attempt to force them into the timeline afterward.

## 16.4 Continuity Rules for AI-Generated Car Footage

Keep these consistent across generated shots:

- Vehicle model and body shape
- Paint color
- Wheel design
- Headlight shape
- Camera side/direction
- Lighting direction
- Weather and road condition
- Time of day
- Motion direction

AI generation is especially prone to changing vehicle geometry between shots. Reject inconsistent frames instead of trying to hide obvious continuity errors with effects.

## 16.5 Quality-Control Checklist

Before export, verify:

- □ Every intentional cut has a rhythmic reason
- □ Main drop contains the strongest visual moment
- □ No distracting AI artifacts on wheels, hands, faces, text, or car geometry
- □ Tracking remains locked during fast movement
- □ Speed ramps do not introduce broken frames or unnatural motion
- □ Shake does not make the footage unreadable
- □ Effects do not hide the subject
- □ Color is consistent across clips
- □ SFX do not overpower the music
- □ Text remains inside safe areas
- □ Final video looks good without headphones
- □ Final video still works when viewed silently
- □ Export settings match the target platform

# 17. AI Editing Agent Behavior

When this skill is used by an AI editing agent, follow this decision loop:

**Analyze → Plan → Rough Cut → Sync → Enhance → VFX → Grade → Sound → QC → Export**

For every requested edit, the agent should identify:

- Target platform
- Target duration
- Phonk subgenre/style
- Source footage quality
- Audio BPM/beat structure
- Key visual moment
- Required effects
- Required AI generation/enhancement
- Output resolution/FPS

### Failure Handling

If AI generation fails, use the original footage or a simpler transition. If tracking fails, switch to manual keyframes. If enhancement creates artifacts, return to the original source. The fallback should always preserve timing and story before preserving an effect.

### Fast Phonk Car Edit Formula

**Strong track → beat markers → best motion shots → rough cut → drop alignment → velocity → controlled shake → motion blur → tracked VFX → dark/high-contrast grade → engine/SFX layer → QC → export.**

The final edit should feel fast because of **timing and motion**, not because every available effect was added.
